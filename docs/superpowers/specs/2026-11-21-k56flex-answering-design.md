# K56flex on the answering side: design

2026-11-21. Status: draft. The owner delegated every decision ("you
implement it and we will test it later"). Each call made here for want of
facts is marked **my call**, with its reason and what it costs if it is
wrong ("Decisions").

## Why

The owner wants the box to serve as many modems as it can. A Rockwell or
Lucent modem of 1997-98 that speaks K56flex but not V.90 (never upgraded,
or upgraded badly) gets V.34 at 33600 at best today. K56flex would give it
32000 to 56000 bit/s down. x2 is done (0.4.0); K56flex is the other
pre-V.90 scheme, and the owner has now bought a K56flex modem (model not
yet known).

The research behind this is in
`~/modem-research/reports/x2 and K56flex protocols.md` and
`~/modem-research/research_notes/x2 and K56flex protocols/k56flex_technical.md`.
The x2 design (`2026-11-20-x2-answering-design.md`) and how x2 was built
(`docs/history/x2-facts.md`, `docs/history/PROGRESS.md` "x2 4" to "x2 9")
are the template.

## Goals

- libv90modem answers a K56flex call as the digital (server) side:
  - Phase 1: V.8bis as the initiator (MRe, CL with the K56flex block,
    the client's MS, ACK(1)), then V.8 selecting V.34;
  - Phase 2: V.34 as the answer modem, K56flex flagged in INFO1a;
  - the server's fixed PCM training, the client's 24-bit J (its pad and
    robbed bits), Phase 4 with V.34 MPs;
  - downstream data at 32000 to 56000 bit/s in steps of 2000, on lines
    with a 0, 3 or 6 dB pad and with robbed bits;
  - the client's in-call rate renegotiations and retrains.
- Upstream is the client's V.34 (it is the V.34 call modem).
- A caller that does not take up K56flex, or whose K56flex start-up stops
  before our INFO1a, gets what it gets today: V.90, V.92, x2, V.34 or
  lower.
- Built and checked before the owner's modem is on the line, so that the
  line tests are confirmation, not development.

## Non-goals

- K56flex on the calling side.
- A-law. No A-law K56flex client has ever been tried; an A-law line gets
  no V.8bis at all.
- Renegotiations or retrains that the server starts itself.
- 58000 and 60000 bit/s (tables exist in SM56; no client selects them).

## Anti-goals: failure even if K56flex works

- Any change to what the answering modem does while K56flex is not
  offered: byte-identical to 0.4.0, replay proof included.
- While it is offered: a V.90, V.92, x2 or V.34 caller that does not take
  it up connects as before (same modulation, rates and data), checked in
  simulation and on live calls before v90d switches it on.
- Code or prose copied from D-Modem (AGPL-3.0) or courier-emu (no
  licence); SM56 binaries or any firmware in the repository.

## Constraints and binding policies (carried over from x2)

- C99, fixed point in the signal path, no allocation after create, no
  global state, identical output on every port target.
- Test-first: each test is seen failing before the code that passes it.
- GPL-2.0-only. **Nothing is copied from D-Modem or courier-emu as code or
  prose.** Facts (signals, fields, timings, sequences, constants) may be
  taken from any source, each cited by URL and commit and tagged in a new
  `docs/history/k56-facts.md` (tags as x2-facts.md: documented, RE
  (D-Modem), our observation, derived).
- **Tables: "Rebuild first, else include."** Derive a table where a rule
  reproduces it (the patents US 6,081,555, 6,259,742, 6,278,744; the V.34
  shell mapper). Otherwise include it as data generated by our own script
  from D-Modem's published values, in a file that names its origin
  (`modem/k56tab.c` from `tools/gen_k56_tables.py`, as `modem/x2bank.c`
  from `tools/gen_x2_banks.py`), listed in `docs/limits.md`.
- Offered only behind a new opt-in bit `V90M_MOD_K56`: answering only,
  needs `V90M_MOD_V34`, mu-law only, not in the defaults.
- After the point of no return (our flagged INFO1a) every failure clears
  down with an end reason, `V90M_END_K56_FAILED`.
- The ARM926 budget (below, "CPU and memory").

## Decisions (my calls)

Each: the call, the reason, the cost if wrong.

1. **Who gets K56flex.** A caller that answers our MRe, returns our block
   in its MS (we ACK(1)), and then in V.8 gets V.34 selected: its CM offers
   V.34 and either no PCM, or PCM while V.90 is not offered here. A CM
   that offers PCM while we offer V.90 gets V.90, exactly as today.
   *Reason:* the owner's x2 ruling ("a caller that offers V.90 gets
   V.90"); D-Modem saw that a JM selecting PCM makes the client run V.90
   and drop K56flex, so V.8 stays the single decision point. *Cost if
   wrong:* a dual-mode client that cannot do V.90 well gets V.90 anyway;
   the owner can force K56 on the client (`+MS=56,0`).
2. **ACK(1) to every MS carrying the K56flex block; never NAK.** The V.8
   JM decides (1). *Reason:* NAK behaviour of real clients is unknown;
   ACK(1) then V.8 is the only path seen working. *Cost:* none known;
   a client that, after ACK(1), refuses a PCM JM would fail V.8 (seen
   on the line test with the modem in automode).
3. **The NS block.** Default: the 9-octet field form `09 b5 02 00 94 81
   83 42 46 e0` (country USA, provider `00 94` K56flex, licensee 81,
   capabilities 83, version 42 with the 0x40 "digital modem" bit, pump 46,
   x-law e0 = mu-law, not forced). A compile-time switch selects SM56's
   proven `07 b5 02 00 94 81 83 61`. If Gough Lui's recording shows a
   real server's CL, its octets replace the default. *Reason:* the field
   values (42/43) are most likely what real servers sent and what a
   hardware Rockwell client saw; both forms pass SM56's stated test
   (`L > 6`, `o[1] b5`, `o[2] 02`, `o[6] & 1`, `(o[7] & 2f) >= 2`,
   `o[7] & 40`). The SM56 peer must accept whichever form ships. *Cost if
   wrong:* the client sends an MS without the block, and the call goes
   V.34 or V.90, as today. Visible in the recording; one constant (or the
   switch, for a test build) fixes it.
   **SM56's acceptance is a gate (my call, from the builder check):** if
   SM56 rejects the 9-octet form in the local harness, the 7-octet `... 83
   61` form becomes the default and the 9-octet one the test-build switch.
   *Reason:* a proven acceptance beats an inference from AT&V fields.
   *Cost if wrong:* a hardware client that wants the field form sends no
   block; the call goes V.34 or V.90, and line test 6 tries the switch.
4. **MRe, not CRe; the timing.** 200 ms of silence, MRe (1375 + 2002 Hz
   400 ms, 650 Hz 100 ms) at the V.8bis level. Then wait for CRd (1529 +
   2225 Hz, then 1900 Hz) until **1000 ms after MRe ends**. Our CL starts
   about 120 ms after CRd ends. Wait for MS until **1500 ms after our CL
   ends**, then ACK(1) (`14`) after a gap of about 180 ms. Then V.8 as
   today, from its 200 ms of silence. With no CRd, or no MS, V.8 follows
   at once, with nothing agreed. **The 1000 ms is a deadline for CRd to
   start (my call):** its dual tone must be detected by then; a CRd that
   has started is received to its end (about 500 ms more). *Reason:* the
   deadline then does not depend on CRd's length, and a slow but valid
   CRd is not cut off. *Cost if wrong:* up to 0.5 s more before ANSam
   for a caller that sends a CRd-like tone and no V.8bis after it.
   **MRe stays 200 ms after we answer (my call).** SM56 answers V.8bis
   only after its blind-dial wait of about 2 s from ATD. On the PSTN the
   ringing covers that; a line that answers with no ringing may not, and
   `docs/limits.md` says so. *Reason:* a longer wait delays every caller;
   the line test shows whether the box's line rings long enough. *Cost if
   wrong:* such a client ignores MRe and connects V.34 or V.90 instead. *Reason:* MRe is the signal SM56 is proven
   on. The observed CRd is 175 ms after MRe and the MS 10 ms after CL;
   the waits leave room for a long round trip without a long delay. *Cost:*
   ANSam comes about 1.5 s later for a caller without V.8bis. For a V.8bis
   caller without K56flex (a V.92 modem answering MRe with quick connect
   and CRd, then no MS) it comes about 3.3 s later. A caller that hangs
   up or misreads in that time is found by the live non-K56 calls before
   v90d enables K56flex.
5. **x2 and K56flex may both be offered.** A K56flex agreement turns x2
   off for that call (no JM flip, no x2 INFO0a bits, no marker hunt).
   Without the agreement, x2 behaves exactly as in 0.4.0. *Reason:* a
   K56flex client is not a U.S. Robotics client; the two decisions are
   made at different points and must not mix. *Cost:* an x2 client that
   answers MRe would lose x2. It is checked on the line with the USR
   modems (`ATS32=66`, both offered).
6. **INFO1a's frequency offset.** When the call is agreed and INFO1c's
   offset (bits 79:88) is `0x001`, INFO1a (bits 40:49) is `0x001`: mu-law,
   x = 0. Anything else in INFO1c makes the call V.34. In an agreed call
   that goes V.34, our measured offset is moved by one step (0.02 Hz)
   when its low two bits are `01`, because the client reads those as
   K56flex. Calls that were not agreed are unchanged. *Reason:* SM56 kept
   K56flex on 1 and 9 and went V.34 on 0, 2, 3, 5, 13, 0x11, 0x200. The
   meaning of x is unknown, so 1 is the value to send. *Cost:* a client
   that needs x = 1 goes V.34, which can be seen on the line.
   **Flag INFO1a only when the upstream symbol rate we select in it is
   3200 (my call, from the builder check);** with any other rate the agreed
   call is plain V.34, unflagged, so the point of no return is not
   reached. *Reason:* the K56flex upstream is seen only at 3200 baud (the
   client's codec at 9600 Hz), and a flagged call at 3000 could leave the
   client unable to send. *Cost if wrong:* on a line too weak for 3200
   up, a client that could have done K56flex at 3000 gets V.34 instead;
   a sim case covers the choice.
7. **The point of no return is our flagged INFO1a.** After it the client
   skips V.34's Phase 3 and waits for our PCM, so there is no way back to
   V.34. Every failure after it clears down with `V90M_END_K56_FAILED`,
   and each wait is bounded (all under 10 s, as in x2d). *Reason:* the
   policy above; SM56 retrains if it gets V.34's Phase 3 instead. *Cost:*
   none beyond a lost call that could not have been saved.
8. **The training is the one SM56 accepts.** Silence 512, R 288, TRN1A
   16368, the client's 3-bit TRN1B 8448, TRN1C 8448, Js 128 (J `0x89b0`,
   16 levels, the only Js SM56 accepts). Then silence until the client's
   24-bit J (12288 symbols at least, rounded up to a multiple of 8). The
   Js we send is always the 16-level one; the "4-level Js" in the open
   points is only whether other clients would take one. Then R
   288, TRN2A 16336, MARK 32, then TRN2B until the client's MP (2048 at
   least, and longer until the data starts on the grid of 24 from TRN1B's
   first symbol). Then MP 704, MP' 704, E 12, and data, B1 first.
   *Reason:* it is the only sequence proven against a shipped client.
   SM56's own unused transmitter has no 3-bit TRN1B and is "possibly an
   older version" (RE). *Cost:* a client that expects the older training
   fails at once after our INFO1a (K56_FAILED). Its recording shows that,
   and the training without TRN1B is then a one-segment change.
9. **The upstream options we ask for.** Our MP asks the client's
   transmitter for 16 states, linear, minimum shaping (trellis, non-linear
   and shaping are for the remote transmitter, V.34 Table 13), as p34d
   does in V.90. The receiver uses the scrambler of V.34's call modem
   (GPC). *Reason:* CPU. x2 costs 2x V.90 only because the x2 client
   dictates 64 states, non-linear encoding and expanded shaping; K56flex
   has a V.34 MP, so we can ask. GPC is D-Modem's server code, "the
   caller's, GPC". The research report's "GPA" for K56flex upstream is
   about the downstream PCM. Step 1 settles it on the SM56 peer. *Cost:*
   a client that ignores the request is not decodable until the receiver
   follows its MP instead (x2d already decodes 64 states, so this is
   configuration), and CPU then rises to x2's.
10. **Rates.** Down: r = min(the client's MP `rate_ac`, our `max_down`,
    13); 30000 + 2000 r bit/s. Our MP names it, and the client takes
    min(ours, its own). Up: the highest rate in the client's MP mask that
    our V.34 receiver serves at its symbol rate, capped by `max_up`.
    *Reason:* the client measures the line, as in SM56's MP. *Cost:* none
    known.
11. **Tables.** K, u (q = u - 1) and r of each rate are checked against
    the frame identity (K + 8q + 8 - 8/r = R / 1000) and the g2, g4, z8
    tables are computed by V.34 9.4, so both are rules. M per pad, the
    ring order (`k56_ring`) and the constellation magnitudes (`k56_bios`)
    get one time-boxed try at a rule: magnitudes as a minimum-distance
    ladder in linear terms under the pad, rings in order of group energy.
    The ring orders published are irregular (for example 2 3 4 5 6 7 1 8
    0 ... at 32000), and the patents say they were built iteratively, so
    the expected result is to include them. They would go into
    `modem/k56tab.c`, generated by `tools/gen_k56_tables.py` from D-Modem's
    `dsp/k56flex_tables.cpp` at `c06a380d` (its numbers only, in our
    layout): 39 ring lists (468 octets) and 39 constellations (1382
    octets), about 2 KB. The pad levels of TRN2B, MP and E (4 per pad, 2
    per pad for renegotiation) are included as facts. *Cost:* none for
    interop. The provenance is the same grey zone as x2's banks, already
    accepted by the owner's rule.
12. **Robbed bits are handled** (x2 does not handle them), because the
    client reports them in its J: on a robbed symbol above 34000 the last
    bit is forced to 0, with the loss L(n) = 0 2 4 4 6 8 8, K' = K - (L -
    n), and 5 robbed symbols counted as 6. The pad selects the table and
    the training levels. *Reason:* the facts are complete, and ATAs and T1
    lines can have robbed bits. *Cost:* code that only the oracle and SM56
    check until a robbed-bit line is found.
13. **Renegotiation: we follow the client's, and never start one.** On its
    S in data mode, we stop at a frame boundary, then send R 288, TRN2A
    2048, MARK 32, TRN2B until its MP, then MP and MP' at 1 bit a symbol
    (2 magnitudes) and E 24, then data on the grid of 24. `v90m_retrain`
    gives `V90M_ESTATE`. *Reason:* after a server's renegotiation SM56's
    data error is 10x and it retrains. *Cost:* an upstream that degrades
    cannot be stepped down from our side; the watchdog ends the call
    (`V90M_END_LOST`). This is revisited once a real modem shows its
    behaviour.
14. **Retrain: we follow the client's and never start one.** Its tone B in
    data mode leads to V.34's retrain Phase 2 (with INFO1a flagged again),
    then the whole training. At most 3 in a row (as `MAX_RETRAINS`), then
    the call clears down. *Reason:* SM56 does not recognise a server's
    tone A as a retrain. Ending the call on every client retrain (as x2
    does) loses calls on lines that real modems would keep. *Cost:* SM56
    fails about half its own retrain trainings (its adapted table), so the
    peer cannot gate this step. The sim client and the line test do.
15. **Cleardown.** `v90m_cleardown` goes silent and ends at once
    (`V90M_END_CLEARDOWN_OURS`), as x2 does: no K56flex cleardown is known.
    A client renegotiation whose MP has rate 0 (V.34 11.7) is
    `V90M_END_CLEARDOWN_FAR`; loss of the upstream is `V90M_END_LOST`.
    *Reason:* V.42's DISC comes first in practice. *Cost:* inferred only;
    the line test shows what the client does.
16. **Memory: everything K56flex goes in `answer_t.u`.** The V.8bis state
    (Phase 1, before any of u's members is in use) and `k56d_t` share the
    union with `v32_t`, `v92d_t` and `x2d_t`. Measured today: u is 45096
    octets (v92d), x2d 20256; k56d (the V.34 upstream receiver and echo
    canceller as x2d's, plus about 1 KB of encoder and training state)
    fits. `answer_t` (106152) gains only flags, and stays below `orig_t`
    (118712). **`v90m_mem_size()` stays 118824 on x86-64 and 118512 on
    ARMv5, and a test pins it.** *Cost if it does not fit:* `V90M_MEM_SIZE`
    has 4056 octets of headroom; past that it grows, which is an API
    change for v90d.
17. **A-law: not offered.** On an A-law line `V90M_MOD_K56` sends no MRe
    (as x2). *Reason:* untried by anyone. *Cost:* none on the owner's mu-law
    box.
18. **Lucent clients are assumed to use the same tables.** K56flex was a
    joint specification, but this is unverified. *Cost:* if the owner's
    modem is a Lucent and the data fails on tables, the recording shows
    it, and nothing else can be done without a Lucent decoder.
19. **Release at "L1" (below) as experimental 0.5.0, off by default.**
    v90d enables it only after the line tests. *Reason:* the owner asked
    for it built now and tested later. The SM56 peer is the strongest
    check possible without hardware. *Cost:* a release that a hardware
    client may still refuse. The labels say so.

## Approaches considered

1. **A new answering path beside x2's (chosen).** It adds:
   - a V.8bis initiator before `v8ans` (`modem/v8bis*`): tone generators
     and detectors, V.21 channel 1 out and channel 2 in, HDLC-like framing
     with FCS-16 (from V.42's HDLC if it fits);
   - INFO1 hooks in `p2ans` / `v34ans`;
   - `modem/k56d` (the call from INFO1a to data, as x2d), `modem/k56tx`
     (the training script, MP, E), `modem/k56enc` (frames of 8 symbols,
     `v34_shell_map`, signs and shaping, robbed bits) and `modem/k56tab`.

   It reuses V.8, V.34 Phase 2, the V.34 upstream receiver (qamrx, ec,
   ecdly, v34train, v34/), V.34's MP (`v34mp`) and the shell mapper
   (`V34_MAX_M` 18 covers M 16).
2. **One "pre-V.90 PCM" engine for x2 and K56flex.** Rejected. Only the
   upstream receiver plumbing is shared; the negotiation, training,
   records or MP and the downstream mapper differ completely, and x2d has
   its Courier proof. Factoring a shared upstream helper out of x2d is
   allowed only if every x2 test and the Courier run stay unchanged.
3. **Port D-Modem's K56flex server.** Excluded by its licence, and it is
   the echo chamber: D-Modem's reading checked against our copy of it.

## The design

### API

- `V90M_MOD_K56` (0x0200): answering only (calling: `V90M_ENOTSUP`), needs
  `V90M_MOD_V34` (without it: `V90M_ENOTSUP`), not in the defaults. The
  test_api "unknown bit" check moves to 0x0400.
- `V90M_CONN_K56FLEX` and `V90M_END_K56_FAILED`, appended.
- `tx_bps` is the downstream payload, 32000 to 56000. Rockwell prints the
  same figure; there is no x2-style offset. `rx_bps` is the V.34 upstream.
- Events as x2: `V90M_EV_DATA_STOPPED`, `V90M_EV_RATES` and
  `V90M_EV_DATA_MOVING` around a renegotiation; `retraining` during a
  retrain.

### Phase 1

As decisions 1 to 5. As the V.8bis initiator the answerer sends CL and
ACK(1) on V.21 channel 1 (980 / 1180 Hz) and hears MS on channel 2 (1650
/ 1850 Hz): the reverse of V.8's answer-side V.21 setup in `v8ans`, which
must not be reused unchanged. MRe, CRd, CL, MS and ACK(1) are V.8bis signals and
messages; our CL is built from V.8bis's field definitions (octet 0
revision 1 and type 2, the identification field's `c9 80 80 80` with 0x40
"NS follows", then the block). An MS is ours when its type is 1 and its
block passes SM56's test. The CRd detector must not fire on anything in
the 106 recorded calls (a check, below).

### Phase 2

V.34's, as answer modem, untouched apart from decision 6: L1 and L2 stay
bit-exact standard, because the client's tandem-codec check reads their
tones near 2100, 3300 and 3750 Hz. Symbol rates are offered as today
(3000, 3200), but INFO1a is flagged only when we select 3200 for the
upstream (decision 6); SM56 used 3200.

### Phases 3 and 4 (`k56d`, `k56tx`)

- Down, decision 8. One GPA scrambler (1 + x^-5 + x^-23) runs from TRN1A
  through E. The codes are the sign in bit 7 (set is positive), with the
  mu-law magnitude index below; the octet is ff - m or 7f - m. TRN1B: 3
  scrambled bits a symbol, magnitudes `40 41 4b 4c 50 51 50 51`, each
  second sign the scrambled bit 23 back, the others against the running
  sum. TRN2B, MP and E: 2 bits a symbol on the 4 magnitudes of the pad
  (none `2f 45 51 57`, 3 dB `35 4d 58 60`, 6 dB `3d 54 61 67`); the sign on
  even symbols is scrambler history bit 24, on odd symbols against the
  running sum. Our MP is V.34's type 0 (88 bits), with `rate_ca`,
  `rate_ac`, a non-zero mask, asymmetric. The levels and sign rules,
  segment by segment, go into `k56-facts.md` before any test is written.
- Up: the client is quiet during our TRN1, and the echo canceller learns
  on it (measuring the echo delay when it is not given), as x2d does on
  its TRN. Then the client sends S 128, S-bar 16, PP 288, TRN, and the
  24-bit J, 4-point and differential: the V.34 J (`0x8990` / `0x89b0`)
  and an octet (bits 0-5: robbed symbol k of 6, counted from TRN1B's
  first symbol; bits 6-7: the pad). Two Js 24 bits apart are the J. We
  must answer it promptly: SM56 waits only about 0.75 s for R. Then J'
  (`0x899f`), TRN (16 points), MP, MP', E, B1.

### Data (`k56enc`)

Frames of 8 symbols, R / 1000 bits each (fewer on robbed bits), in a cycle
of 3 frames. The bits are GPA-scrambled, least significant bit of each
octet first. K' bits go to the shell mapper (V.34 9.4, M rings, shifted
left by L - n when robbed); each symbol gets a sign bit (except every
r-th symbol over the call, whose sign is set against the running sum),
then q bits within its ring. Ring to group via `k56_ring`, point to
magnitude via `k56_bios`. The z8 table saturates at 2^32 for M = 16
(54000), and the 31-bit index stays below it; a test covers it.

### Upstream

V.34 data through qamrx and v34/ as in p34d: GPC, and the options our MP
asked for (decision 9). The watchdogs are p34d's.

### Renegotiation, retrain, cleardown

Decisions 13, 14 and 15.

### CPU and memory

- **Memory:** decision 16.
- **CPU:** V.8bis and the training are negligible. The encoder does one
  shell mapping and 8 lookups a frame, about 1 M instructions a second.
  The upstream is V.90's when the client honours our MP. Target: data mode
  at most 1.2x V.90 answering's 38.9 M instructions a second (`make
  cost-k56` on a sim call, ARMv5 under qemu, as `cost-x2`), so about a
  third of the box. If a real client dictates 64 states and the other
  options, it is x2's 2x (82 M, one call per box), and `limits.md` says so.

SM56 is Motorola's code, independent of D-Modem's reading. The harness
that runs it (its kernel and codec emulation) is D-Modem's. D-Modem is
built and run unmodified; nothing of it, and no binary, enters the repo.

### Proof that nothing else moved

- **K56flex not offered:** the replay of the 106 live-050 recordings with
  flags `""`, `-9`, `-x`, `-x -9` is identical to 0.4.0, `.bits` included;
  every test count is as before.
- **K56flex offered:** the replay cannot prove this case. MRe moves our
  ANSam later, while the recorded caller's CM stays where it was. So:
  - the CRd scan above;
  - sim callers with K56flex offered (`v34peer`, the V.90 and V.92 sim
    callers, `x2call` with x2 also offered) connect with the same
    modulation, rates and data as without it;
  - the live calls before v90d enables it.
- The port matrix once at the end; the fast-verification rules throughout.

## Release labels

- **L0** (sim and D-Modem oracles only): "K56flex (answering, experimental,
  off by default): built from third-party reverse engineering; its octets
  equal D-Modem's server; not tried with any K56flex client." This is all
  that can be claimed until the 32-bit toolchain (`glibc-devel.i686`) is
  installed for the SM56 harnesses; it is released at L0 only if they
  cannot be built.
- **L1** (the SM56 decoder and full call pass): "connects to Motorola's
  SM56 soft modem run under emulation, V.42 data both ways at <rates>,
  with no pad and with robbed bits; its decoder takes our data at every
  rate, pad and robbed-bit pattern; not yet tried on a Rockwell or Lucent
  modem." Release 0.5.0.
- **L2** (the owner's modem): "tried on <model>, <rates>, <pad and robbed
  bits seen>". Only then may v90d offer it by default, and only once the
  live non-K56 calls have passed too.

## Order of work, each step ending in something that works

1. **Facts and harnesses (no product code).** Write
   `docs/history/k56-facts.md` from the sources, segment by segment, with
   V.8bis's field layout checked against the ITU text. Download Gough
   Lui's recording to a directory outside the repository and decode it
   there (MRe or CRe, the CL octets, the training's segment lengths); it
   is never committed. Settle GPC upstream. Make the time-boxed table-rule
   attempt; otherwise write the generator. The implementer clones D-Modem
   at `c06a380d` outside the repository (for example
   `~/modem-research/D-Modem`), builds it there, and runs D-Modem's server
   against SM56 to see the reference call. The SM56 decoder harness and
   `half_sm56` are 32-bit: they need `glibc-devel.i686` (and
   `libgcc.i686`), which the owner installs with sudo. **Until then the
   SM56 gates of steps 4 and 5 cannot run and the release can only be
   L0.**
2. **API and Phase 1.** V.8bis with fall-through, the agreement, x2 off
   when agreed. INFO1a is not flagged yet, so an agreed K56flex caller
   gets V.34 (no point of no return yet). Proof: the replay (not
   offered), the CRd scan, the sim callers. The live non-K56 calls with
   K56flex offered can be made now, before the K56flex modem.
3. **Phase 2 flag.** INFO1a `0x001`, the offset nudge; after the flag,
   `ANS_K56` clears down (`V90M_END_K56_FAILED`) until step 4, as x2 4 did.
4. **Training, Phase 4, data at 32000 with no pad and no robbed bits.**
   `sim/k56call`, the encoder and training oracles, the SM56 decoder
   harness, then the SM56 full call.
5. **Every rate, the pads, robbed bits and the grid of 24.** The oracle
   at all 2496 combinations, SM56's decoder at the same, the SM56 call
   with `NET_RBS`.
6. **The client's renegotiation** (sim client; SM56 when it makes one).
7. **The client's retrain** (sim client; SM56 as information).
8. **Release 0.5.0 at L1.** CHANGELOG, README, DESIGN 4.2b, answering.md,
   limits.md (offered only, A-law, the included tables, no server
   renegotiation or retrain, CPU, and that a line answering with no
   ringing may meet SM56's blind-dial wait), `make cost-k56`, the port
   matrix.
9. **v90d (my call): a separate small v90d change after the 0.5.0
   library release**, copying x2's setting (name pattern, UI, off by
   default). *Reason:* it keeps the library branch to the library and
   follows x2's path. *Cost if wrong:* none beyond a short wait before the
   owner can switch it on for the line tests.

## On the line, later (the owner)

1. **Identify the modem** (a terminal, before any call): `ATI0` to `ATI11`
   (keep all output), `AT+MS=?` and `AT+MS?`. A list with `56` is a
   Rockwell/Conexant K56flex controller (`+MS=56`). No `+MS`, but `S38`
   answers (`ATS38?`), means a Lucent controller (K56flex by `S38`, 1
   automatic or 2-14 a fixed rate, with `S109`). `AT&V` / `AT&V1` (Rockwell)
   or `ATI11` (Lucent / Zoom) after a call is the call report. Tell us the
   chip and firmware string (`ATI3`, for example `V2.210-K56_...`).
2. **Baseline, K56flex not offered:** two calls; it connects V.34 or V.90 as
   any caller would.
3. **K56flex offered, the modem forced to K56flex** (Rockwell `AT+MS=56,0`;
   Lucent `ATS38=1` and V.90 off if it has it): a few calls, recorded. After
   CONNECT, `+++` then `AT&V1` (Flex field, RBS pattern, digital pad,
   "V8bis K56Flex successful", rates, retrain counts). Then one call at each
   fixed rate (`AT+MS=56,0,,,32000,32000` up to 56000, or `S38=2..14`), and
   one long call (10 minutes) for renegotiations and retrains.
4. **Automode** (`AT+MS=56,1`, V.90 left on): it should get V.90 when it
   offers V.90 (decision 1).
5. **The other modems with K56flex offered:** the Conexant and the 5686
   (V.90, V.92), the 1998 USR, and the USR at `ATS32=66` with x2 offered
   too. A few calls each; all must connect as before.
6. If V.8bis fails (no K56flex, the call goes V.34 or V.90), repeat 3 with
   the test build that sends the other NS block (decision 3).

Every call is recorded as for x2; the recordings become fixtures, decoded
field by field, and each open point below is answered from them.

## Not settled by any source (the recordings answer them)

- What a real server sent in V.8bis: MRe or CRe, CL or CLR, the block.
- The meaning of INFO1a's x bit, and of NS octets 3, 4 and 8, 9.
- Whether a hardware client accepts the 3-bit TRN1B, the TRN1C length, a
  4-level Js.
- Lucent tables; A-law; a K56flex cleardown.
- How a real client takes a server's renegotiation or retrain (we start
  neither).
